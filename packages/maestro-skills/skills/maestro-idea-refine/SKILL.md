# Refine an idea before committing to a solution

## Establish the problem

Ask who needs the outcome, what success looks like, constraints and previous attempts. Restate it as
"How might we achieve this outcome for this user without this constraint?" Keep the framing broad enough
for alternatives and narrow enough to test. Do not embed the proposed solution in the question.

## Expand, react, converge

Think widely before presenting selected variations. Choose relevant [frameworks](frameworks.md), rather than
mechanically running every lens. Explore materially different approaches, including a smaller or unconventional
one. Show the selected variations and wait for the person's reaction before narrowing.

Cluster the useful variations into two or three directions. Stress-test each using the
[refinement criteria](refinement-criteria.md): user value, practical feasibility and differentiation.
Separate evidence from assumptions and expose dealbreakers before implementation. The user's reaction may
change the framing; return to exploration when the directions solve the wrong problem.

For deeper requirements, suggest `/skill:maestro-grilling` to the human. Facts that need primary-source work
can use `/skill:maestro-research`. The controller owns research dispatch; a child reports the open question
instead of starting another agent. Do not turn type-only suggestions into automatic invocations.

## Confirm a one-pager

Present the chosen direction with:

- Problem, specific user and desired outcome.
- Proposed approach and why it wins over the other directions.
- Assumptions and the cheapest validation that could disprove the riskiest one.
- One-job MVP within the available time and a Not Doing list with reasons.
- Open questions and the next experiment.

Ask for confirmation before saving the one-pager. Hypothetical metrics or imagined reactions remain labelled
as such; neither enthusiasm nor a generated example is evidence of demand.
